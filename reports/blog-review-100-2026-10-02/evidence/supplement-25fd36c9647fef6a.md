# Dashes and hyphens
Since an en dash (–, half the width of one [em](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Em_\(typography\))) is not readily available on a usual keyboard while a hyphen (-, quarter the width of one em) is, the two are often confused. This is especially unacceptable in a LaTeX document since one can type two hyphens (--) to make an en dash (and --- to make an em dash).
En dashes are for juxtaposing different things, e.g. 
  * [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy%E2%80%93Schwarz_inequality)
  * [local–global principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse_principle)
  * [East–West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism)

or for ranges of values, e.g. 
  * Alexander Grothendieck (1928–2014)
  * pp. 349–366
  * chapters 2–4


Hyphens are for compound nouns and adjectives, e.g. 
  * R-algebra
  * 2-dimensional
  * user-friendly

or for compound proper names, e.g. 
  * [Shiing-Shen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiing-Shen_Chern)
  * [Swinnerton-Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Swinnerton-Dyer)
  * [Baden-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Baden)

If we confuse the two, how would we know if the [BSD conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch_and_Swinnerton-Dyer_conjecture) is due to Birch, Swinnerton and Dyer, Birch-Swinnerton and Dyer, or Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer?
